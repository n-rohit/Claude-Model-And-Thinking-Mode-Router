One of our banking clients is planning to buy a smaller regional
lender, and their board asked us to independently verify the target's
financial health before anyone signs anything.

The client has handed over five years of financial statements, board
meeting minutes, and letters exchanged with their central bank about
past compliance findings. They want us to go through all of it and
tell them:
1. Whether the target's reported numbers actually line up year over
   year, or if there are unexplained swings worth asking about
2. Any pattern in the central bank correspondence that suggests
   recurring problems rather than one-off issues
3. Whether the target's loan book looks concentrated in a way that
   could be risky if one sector has a bad year
4. What we'd flag as a dealbreaker versus something that's just worth
   a lower purchase price

This needs to hold up if the client's board asks hard questions in the
approval meeting, so every conclusion should point back to something
specific in the documents, not a general impression. If the numbers
genuinely look clean, say so plainly rather than manufacturing
concerns to seem thorough.
